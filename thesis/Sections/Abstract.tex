\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Single-cell foundation models promise \emph{in silico} screening of drug perturbations, and the
Cell2Sentence family makes the promise concrete by serialising a cell's transcriptome as a sentence
of gene symbols and modelling it with a pretrained language model. This thesis fine-tunes
\texttt{C2S-Scale-Pythia-1b} on Tahoe-100M, the largest available drug-perturbation atlas, and asks
whether the resulting model captures drug-specific biology.

The evaluation turns out to be the first result. A zero-information baseline that assigns every gene
the middle rank scores $0.9999$ on the field's standard differential-expression correlation --- above
the two-replicate noise ceiling and above the model. Grading the metrics rather than the models under
a Dynamic Range Fraction analysis, only a discrimination metric survives calibration; three of four
prediction metrics rank an uninformative baseline above the noise ceiling.

On that calibrated metric the model is drug-blind: four independent instruments place it at chance
and statistically indistinguishable from a prompt carrying the wrong drug, even on the third of
conditions that are identifiable in principle. The failure is not the representation --- true
expression discriminates drugs no better than rank at single-cell resolution --- and it is not the
training target, since an optimal-transport family whose endpoints are two independently tested
target constructions leaves the model in the near-null regime throughout. Probing localises it
instead to the readout: drug identity becomes \emph{more} linearly decodable with depth, reaching
$0.88$ by layer 16 once cellular context is projected out, while the causal variance share of that
same subspace stays flat near $3\%$.

The bottleneck is the tokenisation. Because a cell sentence ranks genes by expression, $83\%$ of the
target tokens are identical between any two drugs in the same context, so cross-entropy receives
roughly $1\%$ of its gradient from drug identity. Re-encoding the target as the drug-specific
residual raises that fraction to $59\%$ and produces the first non-null drug effect in this work,
which transfers to (drug, cell line) pairings never seen in training with no memorisation premium.

It is nonetheless beaten by a lookup table --- a per-drug mean residual measured in other cell lines
scores $0.963$ against a $0.968$ ceiling, where the model reaches $0.639$. A noise-corrected variance
decomposition explains why: the response is a per-drug constant plus a drug$\times$cell-line
interaction worth roughly $45\%$ of the drug-specific variance, and that interaction has no
consistent per-cell-line structure. Being unpredictable, it acts as irreducible noise for any
predictor, so the lookup is already at the \emph{achievable} ceiling and nothing can beat retrieval
on drugs it has seen. For drugs it has not, protein-target and mechanism annotations do carry
transferable signal where chemical structure does not --- overturning a pre-registration made in this
work. But decomposing the prompt ablation by field shows the model reads the drug \emph{name} and
does not measurably read the \emph{mechanism} it is already given, so the constraint there is the
readout rather than the information.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} single-cell transcriptomics, perturbation prediction, drug response,
language models, benchmark calibration, mechanistic interpretability.
