\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Cell-sentence models recast an expression-ranked transcriptome as text and use a language model to
predict a treated cell from an untreated cell and a named perturbation. This thesis asks whether a
fine-tuned C2S-Scale Pythia-1b checkpoint learns enough drug-specific biology from Tahoe-100M for
useful condition-specific prediction. The evaluation is audited before the model is judged. Of five
candidate metrics, only normalised inverse rank (\NIR) rewards a real replicate more than a
zero-information predictor: its Dynamic Range Fraction is $+0.635$ across plates, $+0.545$ within
plate, and $+0.704$ in a separately calibrated residual frame. The other four prediction metrics are
negative in both expression-frame audits; they were not re-audited in the residual frame. This shows
that apparently strong scores can reward generic expression rather than the identity of the drug.

On unseen drugs in the expression frame, the original single-cell-target checkpoint scores $0.498$
against $0.504$ for a control-copy baseline and a $0.576$ within-well split-half precision reference.
Restricting the analysis to empirically identifiable conditions does not reveal a useful advantage.
The target itself is part of the difficulty: standard treated profiles preserve extensive
cell-state overlap across drugs, whereas encoding the drug-specific residual exposes substantially
more drug-dependent token variation. The residual-target checkpoint shows sensitivity to
trained drug identity, beating a geometry-matched prompt scramble by $+0.0898$ on trained
conditions. That sensitivity does not establish transfer. The held-out combination estimate depends
on whether uncertainty is clustered over cell lines or drugs, and the unseen-drug estimate is
$-0.0315$ with intervals spanning zero on both axes.

The decisive baseline is simpler than generation. On the $1{,}192$ conditions where both are
defined, a training-only lookup of each drug's mean residual in other cell lines scores $0.913$,
against $0.549$ for the model. Yet this does not make the response a pure per-drug constant. A
disattenuated cross-context transfer coefficient is $\Tcoef=0.553$
\nolinebreak[3]$[0.514,0.593]$; its complement is not an interaction or variance share because the
estimator mixes cell line, dose, well, and target-estimation differences. A channel gate suggests
that protein-target annotation is the strongest lead for drugs absent from fine-tuning, but the
held-out support is too small to establish unseen-drug generalisation. The central conclusion is
therefore narrower than successful conditioning: prompt sensitivity is established, biological
transfer is not. The thesis contributes a calibrated evaluation protocol, a strong retrieval bar,
and explicit bounds on what this checkpoint learned and what remains unresolved.
